# Shiba animation storyboard

How every part of the dog moves, so it reads as a real puppy and as the Nintendogs puppy. This is the authoring spec: numbers here go straight into `blender/author_clips.py` pose functions and into the Mac runtime layers. Nothing here is hand-keyed.

Priority order, per David: **walk and run**, **head petting (eyes closed, mouth open, soothed)**, **tail carriage and wag driven by mood**, then everything else.

## 0. Conventions

- Rig: 28 joints, rigid skinning, faces **+Z**. Seam joints `tail1`, `neck`, `Lleg1`/`Rleg1`, `Larm1`/`Rarm1` stay ≤ 7° (existing tier-2 limit). Range goes to the next joint down the chain.
- Angles are degrees in the pose-function convention already used in `author_clips.py`:
  - `head` pitch **+ = nose down**, − = nose up. Yaw + = turn to the dog's left. Roll = head tilt.
  - `mouth` pitch + = jaw open (18° bark, 30° yawn; 45° is the mesh limit before the jaw interior shows through the muzzle).
  - `Lear1`/`Rear1` pitch **− = folded back**, + = pricked forward. Roll = splay outward.
  - `tail2`/`tail3` pitch + = tail lowered, − = raised. Yaw = side-to-side wag.
  - `root_location` y + = up. Walk/run: x and z stay 0 (root travel is the app's job); a small y bob is allowed (see §2.6).
- Timing: 30 fps. `t` is the normalized clip time 0…1. Curves: `ease` is the smoothstep already in the script; "pulse(a,b)" is the sin² bump between a and b.
- Two kinds of motion:
  - **Clips** (Blender-exported, all 28 joints): posture and locomotion. Regenerated by `author_clips.py`.
  - **Runtime layers** (SceneKit, additive on top of the clip): tail wag, blink/eye state, breathing, ear micro-moves, gaze, jaw pant, tongue. These are continuous and mood-driven, which a fixed clip cannot be. They compose the way gaze already does: `SCNTransformConstraint`, non-incremental, rotation only, joint origin preserved.

## 1. Mood model (drives the layers)

The runtime keeps three scalars, updated each tick from needs, interaction history and the current state. Layers read them; clips do not.

| Scalar | Range | Rises with | Falls with | Half-life |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `arousal` | 0…1 | call, hover, ball, greeting, bark | time, sleep | 8 s |
| `valence` | −1…1 | petting (+0.15/stroke), feeding, play, greeting | hunger/thirst critical (−), scolding-free, so rarely negative | 60 s |
| `energy` | 0…1 | rest, sleep | time awake, running | needs-driven (existing) |

Derived moods used below:

| Mood | Condition | Tail | Ears | Mouth | Eyes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Calm** | arousal < 0.3, valence ≥ 0 | sickle curl, slow sway | neutral | closed | blink 4–6 s |
| **Content** | valence > 0.4, arousal < 0.5 (after petting/food) | high curl, medium wag, right-biased | slightly back | soft pant | slow blink, squint |
| **Excited** | arousal > 0.6, valence > 0.3 (call, greeting, ball) | high, fast wide wag, whole rear wiggles | pricked forward | open pant, tongue out | wide (no squint) |
| **Alert** | arousal > 0.6, valence ≈ 0 (noise, cursor appears) | raised, **still** | pricked forward, rotate to source | closed | wide, no blink 2 s |
| **Low** | energy < 0.25 or hunger/thirst < 0.3 | lowered, hangs, no wag | back and down | closed | half-lidded, long blinks |
| **Asleep** | sleep state | flat on ground | relaxed | closed | closed |

Real-dog note: wagging is a social signal; a dog alone in a room hardly wags. So the wag envelope also scales with `cursorNearby` (0.6 alone → 1.0 with the cursor in the panel), which is the desktop analogue of "the owner is here".

## 2. Walk (`shiba_walk_loop`, 40 frames = 1.33 s per stride cycle)

A puppy walk is a four-beat lateral-sequence gait: **LH, LF, RH, RF**, each footfall a quarter cycle apart. Each foot spends ~65 % of the cycle on the ground (stance) and ~35 % in the air (swing). Three feet are down most of the time; the body never leaves the ground.

### 2.1 Footfall timing (phase offsets, fraction of the cycle)

| Limb | Swing starts | Swing ends / touchdown | Stance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Left hind `Lleg` | 0.00 | 0.35 | 0.35–1.00 |
| Left fore `Larm` | 0.25 | 0.60 | 0.60–1.25 |
| Right hind `Rleg` | 0.50 | 0.85 | 0.85–1.50 |
| Right fore `Rarm` | 0.75 | 1.10 | 1.10–1.75 |

The existing script uses a 50/50 swing/stance sine. Change to a **duty factor of 0.65**: swing is `phase ∈ [0, 0.35)`, stance is the rest. This is the single biggest "looks like a real dog" fix after the direction bug.

### 2.2 Foot path

Paw target relative to rest, in body space (z forward):

- **Stance** (65 %): paw slides straight back at constant speed, from `+stride/2` to `−stride/2`. Height 0 (on the floor). This constant backward speed is what `GaitStride` measures; it must be linear, not sinusoidal, or the dog surges.
- **Swing** (35 %): paw travels forward from `−stride/2` to `+stride/2` along an arc: height `lift · sin(π·s)` where `s` is swing progress 0…1, with the peak at `s = 0.4` (paw lifts fast, lands soft — skew the sine: `sin(π·s^0.8)`).
- Stride: fore 0.17, hind 0.23 (existing values are right; hind steps land slightly inside the fore print).
- Lift: fore 0.10, hind 0.13. Puppies pick up their feet a little high.
- Paw pitch (`*hand2`/`*foot2`): during the last 20 % of swing the paw rotates toe-down 15° so the toes touch first; during the last 15 % of stance the heel lifts (toe-off) with the paw rotating +20°.

The two-link IK in the script turns the paw target into knee/hock and elbow/wrist angles; keep the bend reserve.

### 2.3 Limb roots (seam joints, ≤ 7°)

`Lleg1`/`Rleg1` (hips) and `Larm1`/`Rarm1` (shoulders) swing ±4° in phase with the limb (forward at touchdown, back at toe-off). This is small but it moves the thigh silhouette, which is what the eye reads as "the leg is walking".

### 2.4 Body

- **Vertical bob** (`root_location.y`): two bumps per cycle, +0.012 at each fore touchdown (0.60 and 1.10), −0.008 at mid-stance. Tiny. Allowed now for walk/run as y-only; `author_clips.py`'s root assertion changes to check x and z only.
- **Roll** (`waist` roll): ±2°, the rear rolls toward the hind leg in stance. **Yaw** (`waist` yaw): ±2° opposite to the hind swing (hips rotate toward the swinging leg).
- **Chest/spine** (`spin` pitch): ±1° with the bob.
- **Head**: `neck` 0, `head` pitch bob +2° down at each fore touchdown, yaw ±2° at half the cycle rate (the head drifts a little side to side as the shoulders alternate). Head looks straight ahead; the gaze layer adds interest on top.
- **Ears**: pricked +3° while walking (the dog is going somewhere), with a random flick every 2–4 s from the ear layer.
- **Tail**: `tail2` yaw ±5° at the cycle rate (hips drive it), phase-lagged 0.15 behind `waist` yaw; `tail3` lags another 0.15. Carriage from mood (§4).
- **Mouth**: closed. Pant layer may add on top when Excited/Content.

### 2.5 Speed

Root travel is measured from the clip (`GaitStride`): with the 0.65 duty factor and linear stance the measured speed is the true stride speed. Do not multiply it. Puppy walk ≈ 0.7 body-heights/s in the normalized model, which the current clip already gives.

Starting and stopping: the app fades idle → walk over 0.25 s. To hide the fade, the walk clip's frame 1 must be a pose with three feet planted and the left hind just lifting, which the phase table above guarantees. Stopping: fade back to idle at any point; the fade is short enough that the paw slide is invisible. A dedicated "stop" clip is not worth it.

### 2.6 Turning

The app rotates `petAnchor` yaw with a 0.3 s ease. Real dogs turn by stepping, which the walk clip already shows. Add: while turning, the runtime layers bank the head 4° into the turn and lag it 100 ms behind the body so the head "leads" the turn visually. No clip changes.

## 3. Run (`shiba_run_loop`, 28 frames = 0.93 s per cycle)

Puppies rarely gallop indoors; the Nintendogs run is a **bouncy trot/canter**: diagonal pairs with a brief moment of suspension. Timing:

| Limb | Swing | Stance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left fore `Larm` + right hind `Rleg` | 0.00–0.45 | 0.45–1.00 |
| Right fore `Rarm` + left hind `Lleg` | 0.50–0.95 | 0.95–1.50 |

Duty factor **0.55** (each foot on the ground just over half the time); both diagonals are off the ground for ~5 % at 0.45–0.50 and 0.95–1.00. Stride fore 0.24, hind 0.32; lift fore 0.16, hind 0.20.

Body: vertical bob **+0.04** at each suspension (0.47 and 0.97), trough −0.02 at mid-stance; `spin` pitch −3° (chest up) at suspension, +2° at mid-stance; `waist` counter-pitches by half. Head pitch −4° (nose up) at suspension so the head floats, +3° at landing. Ears back −6° (wind). Tail: raised −10° pitch, yaw ±8° at the cycle rate; the run is the one place the tail is a rudder, not a signal. Mouth open 8° with the tongue quad visible from the second cycle onward (panting starts once the dog has been running).

Hips/shoulders ±6° (near the limit; verify).

## 4. Tail

The single most expressive part. Runtime layer on `tail2` and `tail3` (never `tail1`), additive on the clip's own tail pose.

### 4.1 Carriage (slow, from mood; eased 0.8 s)

| Mood | `tail2` pitch | `tail3` pitch | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calm | −8 | −14 | Shiba sickle: curls up and over the back. This is the rest carriage. |
| Content | −12 | −18 | Higher, tighter curl. |
| Excited | −14 | −16 | High; tail3 relaxes as the wag speeds up. |
| Alert | −16 | −20 | Highest, rigid. |
| Low | +25 | +10 | Hangs. |
| Asleep / lying | clip owns it | | Layer weight → 0 in lying states. |

### 4.2 Wag

`tail2` yaw = `A · sin(2π f t + bias)`, `tail3` yaw = `0.55 · A · sin(2π f t − 0.9)` (tail3 lags 0.9 rad, which is the whip that makes it look flexible, not a stiff paddle).

| Mood | A (deg) | f (Hz) | Side bias | Envelope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calm | 6 | 0.7 | 0 | continuous, 60 % of the time; pauses 3–8 s |
| Content | 16 | 2.0 | +4° right | continuous while cursor near; bursts of 2–3 s otherwise |
| Excited | 24 | 4.0 | +6° right | continuous; `waist` yaw ±3° in phase (the rear wiggles) |
| Alert | 0 | — | — | still for the first 2 s, then Calm |
| Low | 3 | 0.4 | −3° left | rare single swings |
| Petting burst | 22 | 3.5 | +6° right | 1.5 s burst on each stroke, decays into Content |

Real-dog detail: a happy dog wags with a rightward bias (left-hemisphere approach behaviour), a worried one leftward. The bias is a constant offset on `tail2` yaw. It is subtle and it is correct.

Rules: the wag layer's weight fades to 0 over 0.3 s while a clip that owns the tail plays (`happy`, `pet_react`, `fetch_pickup`), and back to 1 after. Frequency changes ease over 0.5 s so the tail never snaps between tempos. Frame-rate independence: the phase accumulates from `deltaTime`, never from wall-clock.

## 5. Head petting

The core soothing loop. Two parts: a body clip and a face state.

### 5.1 First stroke (`shiba_pet_react`, existing, 75 frames) → replace with `shiba_pet_head_loop` (60 frames, loop) after the first 0.5 s

Timeline from the stroke that starts petting:

| Time | Head | Ears | Eyes | Mouth | Tail | Body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0 s | current pose | | open | | | |
| 0.0–0.3 | pitch −6 (nose up into the hand), roll toward the hand 8° | fold back −18 over 0.3 s | blink closed at 0.15 s | | burst wag | weight shifts: `spin` pitch −1 |
| 0.3–0.6 | head pushes further up: pitch −10, small yaw toward the cursor (gaze layer, capped 12°) | stay back | **closed** (eye_closed texture) | opens to 12° with a soft ease — the "smile" | Content wag, 2.5 Hz | |
| 0.6 → loop | `shiba_pet_head_loop`: head bobs ±2° at 0.6 Hz (leaning into each stroke), roll ±3° | −18 with a ±2° flutter at 1.2 Hz | closed; every 4–6 s a half-open squint for 0.4 s (eye_squint texture) then closed again | 12° open, jaw ±1.5° at 1.5 Hz (relaxed pant); tongue quad visible from 2 s onward, 20 % out | Content, right-biased | breathing layer at 1.4× rate |

Each new stroke while looping: head pitch dips +4° for 0.2 s then recovers (the hand pressing), tail burst. The dog visibly leans toward the cursor's side.

### 5.2 Settling (sustained petting > 6 s, or 3 strokes within 4 s)

`shiba_pet_settle` (90 frames, no loop): standing → sit → tips onto its **right** side (the side away from the cursor; if the cursor is on the dog's right, tip left).

| Frames | Motion |
| --- | --- |
| 1–30 | Rear lowers into the existing `SIT` pose (blend 0→1, ease). Head stays up in the hand, eyes closed, mouth open. |
| 30–55 | Hindquarters slide out to the side: `waist` roll 20° (limit; put the rest in root), `root_location` rolls the whole dog about z by 75° and lowers y to −0.70, `Lleg2`/`Rleg2` extend to −40°, hind paws point sideways. Fore legs fold: `Larm2` −30, `Lhand1` 25. Head lowers last, pitch +30, rests on the floor at frame 55. |
| 55–90 | Relax: every joint eases 10 % back toward rest (a "sigh"), ears relax to −8, mouth closes to 4°, one slow blink then closed. Ends held. |

Then `shiba_lie_side_loop` (120 frames): breathing on `chest` 1.5° at 0.4 Hz, hind paw twitch at frame 70 (`Lfoot2` −10 for 6 frames), ear flick at frame 40, tail tip (`tail3` only) lazy ±4° at 0.5 Hz. Petting the belly during this loop → `shiba_belly_loop` (90 frames): root roll to 170° (on the back), all four legs bent 30–50° and pointing up, gentle alternating paw curl (`*hand2` ±8° at 0.8 Hz, left/right out of phase), mouth 25° open, head turned yaw 15° toward the cursor, tongue 40 % out. Eyes closed.

Petting stops for > 5 s → `shiba_lie_side_up` (45 frames): head lifts first (frames 1–15), then the fore legs plant and the body rolls back to sitting (15–40), ends on `sit_idle` frame 1. Eyes open at frame 10 with a blink.

### 5.3 Face during petting: the "soothed" look

This is what makes it read as Nintendogs. Three things at once, none of which the current build does:

1. **Eyes closed** — texture swap (§7.1). Eyes close *before* the mouth opens (0.15 s vs 0.3 s). Closing first is what says "enjoying", opening the mouth first would say "panting".
2. **Mouth open, jaw relaxed** — 12°, not the bark's 18°. The atlas already has the pink interior with a painted tongue, so the open jaw shows it.
3. **Ears back** — −18°, symmetric. Ears back + eyes closed + tail wag = pleasure. (Ears back + eyes wide + tail low would be fear; the combination matters.)

## 6. Idle life (runtime layers, always on)

- **Breathing**: `chest` pitch ±0.5° at 0.5 Hz standing, ±0.7° at 0.35 Hz lying/sleeping; `spin` 40 % of that, lagged 0.3 rad. Rate ×1.4 when Excited (panting) and ×0.7 asleep. Additive; the idle clip's own 120-frame breath is removed from the clip when this layer lands so they don't beat against each other.
- **Blink**: eye_closed for 3 frames (100 ms), interval random 3–7 s (mean 4.5 s), never during Alert's first 2 s, doubled while Low. 15 % of blinks are doubles (two blinks 250 ms apart).
- **Ear flick**: random ear, `pitch` −10 → 0 over 6 frames, every 4–12 s. 30 % chance the other ear follows within 0.5 s. Ears also *track* stimuli: both ears rotate yaw up to 20° toward the cursor when it moves fast, 0.5 s after the head starts to turn (ears lead the head in real dogs; here the head is gaze-driven so the ears follow 0.5 s to feel deliberate).
- **Weight shift**: every 8–20 s standing, `waist` roll ±1.5° eased over 1 s, held 3–6 s. Makes a still dog look like it is standing, not parked.
- **Lick / nose**: every 30–90 s while Calm: `mouth` opens 6° for 4 frames, tongue quad flicks out 60 % for 6 frames (nose lick). Puppies do this constantly.
- **Pant**: when Excited or after > 4 s of running: `mouth` 8° + 4° at 3.5 Hz, tongue out 30 % with a ±10 % bounce at the pant rate, head nods ±1° in phase. Fades over 3 s once arousal drops.

## 7. New elements

### 7.1 Eye states (texture swap on the `eye` material)

The eye is its own 32×32 texture (`eye_0.png`) on its own material, which `ModelLoader` already assigns by name. Three more textures, same size, same UV, swapped by setting `material.diffuse.contents`:

| File | Look | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `eye_0.png` | existing open eye | default |
| `eye_closed.png` | fur-colored patch, one thin dark curved line, curving **down** at the outer corner | blink, petting, sleep, belly |
| `eye_squint.png` | eye_0 with the upper third covered by a fur-colored lid, lower edge slightly curved | content squint, sleepy, waking |
| `eye_wide.png` | eye_0 with the iris 10 % smaller and a larger white highlight | Alert, call stun, ball in the air |

Make them from `eye_0.png` in any image editor or with an AI tool: the prompt is "32×32 pixel game texture, closed dog eye on black-and-tan shiba fur, single thin dark line curving down at the outer corner, matches the attached open-eye texture's fur color and pixel style, no anti-aliased blur". Keep the fur pixels around the border identical to `eye_0.png` so the seam does not show. Coat A and C get their own copies (fur color differs).

Transition rule: the swap is instant (a real blink is instant); the *timing* is what is animated (§6).

### 7.2 Tongue

The mesh has no tongue geometry; the pink interior is painted on the mouth. Solution: a small SceneKit plane (`SCNPlane`, 0.12 × 0.06 model units, double-sided, `tongue.png` 64×32 with alpha), parented to the `mouth` joint, origin at the jaw hinge, rotated so it lies along the lower jaw and pokes out the front. Hidden by default (`isHidden`); shown by the pant/pet/belly states with an "out" amount that translates it forward along the jaw (0 = tucked, 1 = 0.05 out past the lip) and a ±10 % bounce at the pant rate. Slight pitch down (10°) so it drapes over the lower teeth. Because it is parented to `mouth`, it opens and closes with the jaw for free. `tongue.png`: pink, darker center line, rounded tip, soft alpha edge — AI prompt: "top-down small pink dog tongue sprite, rounded tip, subtle center groove, flat shading, transparent background, 64×32". Coat-independent.

### 7.3 Mouth interior

Already there. Jaw > 8° shows it. No new asset.

## 8. Call (whistle) sequence

Click on the panel (not on the dog) = call. Ordered states, each hands off on the previous pose:

| # | State | Frames | Body | Head | Ears | Eyes | Tail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `shiba_call_stun` | 15 | Freeze: whatever clip is playing fades over 0.1 s to a held pose | pitch −5 | snap to +10 forward in 3 frames | eye_wide | **stops** mid-wag (layer weight 0) |
| 2 | `shiba_call_curious` | 40 | still | yaw toward the camera 25° then roll 15° tilt at frame 12, hold, roll back at frame 30 | one ear +12 forward, the other 0 | wide → open at frame 25 | still |
| 3 | run | app | `shiba_run_loop` with root travel to the near limit (z ≈ 0) | gaze layer at the cursor | back −6 (wind) | open | rudder (§3) |
| 4 | `shiba_paws_up_enter` | 30 | rear into `SIT`; `Larm2`/`Rarm2` −70 (elbows up), `Lhand1`/`Rhand1` +40, paws flat toward camera at chest height | pitch −15 (looking up at you), yaw 0 | +6 forward | open | Excited |
| 5 | `shiba_paws_up_loop` | 90 | paws alternately push 0.02 forward (left at 0.2, right at 0.6), `spin` −2 | bob ±2 at 1 Hz, head follows the cursor (gaze) | +6 | open, blink | Excited, `waist` wiggle ±3 |
| 6 | `shiba_paws_down` | 30 | paws lower, settle exactly on `sit_idle` frame 1 | | | | Content |

Stun timing matters: a real dog freezes for ~0.5 s on an unexpected sound before orienting. 15 frames.

## 9. Sleep and waking

Existing clips are right in shape. Add from the layers: eyes closed the whole time; breathing 0.35 Hz, deeper; every 20–60 s a paw twitch (`Lhand2` −12 for 4 frames) or a brief ear flick — dreaming. Waking (`shiba_wake`): eyes stay closed until frame 20, open to squint for 1 s, then open; one big blink at the end of the stretch.

## 11. Acceptance (what "done" looks like on screen)

- Walking: nose leads, three feet down at any moment, feet do not slide, the head bobs, the rear sways. Filmed side-on at 0.5× speed it reads as a walk, not a shuffle.
- Running: visible bounce, a moment with all feet up, ears back, tongue out after two cycles.
- Petting: eyes shut *first*, then the mouth opens into the smile, ears fold, tail goes into a right-biased wag; the head leans into the cursor. Keep stroking and the dog sits, flops, and the wag never stops until it is fully down.
- Tail: never static while awake and Calm; speeds up the moment you interact; goes still and high when startled; hangs when hungry.
- Standing still for 30 s the dog blinks, shifts weight, flicks an ear and licks its nose at least once. Nothing repeats on a visible cycle.
